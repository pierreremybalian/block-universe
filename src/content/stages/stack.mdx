Take a film of the world and, instead of playing it, cut out the frames and stack them. Each frame is one moment: where everything was at that instant. The stack as a whole is the history of everything in the film.

In 1908 Hermann Minkowski told an audience in Cologne that “space by itself, and time by itself, are doomed to fade away into mere shadows, and only a kind of union of the two will preserve an independent reality.” The union is spacetime, and relativity is written in it.

In spacetime a thing that lasts is not a point but a line, its worldline. Something at rest is a vertical line. Something circling is a helix. Something speeding past is a line that leans. Nothing in the block moves; motion is a matter of shape.

This block is drawn to the shared scale used throughout: one unit across is one light-hour, one unit up is one hour. The star and planet are schematic. A real planet takes months or years to go around once, so its helix would climb far more steeply than shown.
