Ask anyone what time is and you will usually get some version of this picture. There is a present moment, it is the same everywhere, and it moves. Yesterday is gone, tomorrow is not here yet, and the only things that exist are the things that exist now.

Philosophers call this presentism. It fits experience well: we remember the past but cannot visit it, we plan for the future but cannot see it, and everything we touch, we touch now.

The picture contains a hidden assumption that turns out to matter a great deal. For “now” to be the same everywhere, there has to be one correct answer to the question “what is happening at this moment on a planet far away?” The next stages build the tools to check that assumption against the physics of light and clocks.
