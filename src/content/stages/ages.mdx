On a map, the points one kilometer from a town form a circle. In spacetime, the events one year of travel from an event form a hyperboloid: a bowl that opens upward inside the light cone. Its bottom is the event a year directly above the start, reached by staying still. Every other point is reached by moving, so it lies farther away in space and later in the starting frame’s time, yet the traveler’s clock reads the same one year.

The bowl is invariant. A boost slides events along it but never off it, so every observer draws the same surface. That makes it a better reference than any “now”: it is built from proper time, which all observers agree on.

Each traveler’s plane of simultaneity touches the bowl at exactly one point, their own birthday, and leans by their speed. The point of contact is the only place where a traveler’s now and the bowl agree. Different travelers touch the bowl at different points with differently tilted planes. There is no single plane that touches it everywhere, which is another way of saying that there is no universal now.
