The arguments in the earlier stages lead to the block universe only if you accept one premise: that what is simultaneous with something real, for any observer, is itself real. Reject that premise and other views of time stay open.

Presentists say only the present exists. Relativity makes this awkward, since there is no observer-independent present to pick out, but some presentists accept a privileged frame that no experiment can detect. Growing-block theorists say the past and present exist and the future does not; the edge of reality advances. George Ellis’s “evolving block” is a version that ties the growing edge to quantum events. Moving-spotlight views keep the whole block but add an objective, moving now.

The block universe needs none of these extras. Its cost is that the flow of time we experience has to be explained as a feature of how we are built, through memory and the arrow of entropy, rather than as a feature of reality. Which price to pay is still debated.
