In the block, a person is a long, thin thread. It is not a path traced out by a moving point; the whole thread exists, birth at one end and old age at the other. A worldline like this is sometimes called a “space-time worm.”

Relativity also says the thread is not quite one line. Different parts of you sit at different heights in Earth’s gravity, and clocks higher up run faster. Over a lifetime your head out-ages your feet by about half a microsecond. That is far too small to notice, but it is the same effect that a pair of optical clocks detected when one was lifted by a third of a meter.

None of this explains why time feels as if it flows in one direction. The usual answer is entropy. The block is not the same at both ends: in our universe, entropy is low toward the Big Bang and rises toward the future. Records, including the traces in a brain that we call memories, can only form in one direction along that slope. So every moment remembers its past and none remembers its future, even though, on the block view, both are equally there.
