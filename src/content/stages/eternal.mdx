Putnam’s 1967 paper starts from the everyday view that what exists is what exists now, and adds one demand from relativity: no observer’s now is privileged. Anything simultaneous with you, for someone standing where you stand, has to count as existing. Since observers moving at different speeds tilt their nows differently, that already makes real every event outside your light cone.

The second step reaches inside the light cone. An event in your future is never simultaneous with you, for anyone at your location. But it is simultaneous with some event elsewhere that is simultaneous with you. If “exists” is shared, so that what exists for someone who exists also exists for you, your future exists. Run the same step backward and so does your past.

Nothing in the step is special to the lamps in this picture. From any event, slices in every direction reach every other event in at most two steps. The result is the block: one four-dimensional whole in which every moment is as real as every other, and in which “now” picks out a location, as “here” does, rather than the boundary of what exists.

Einstein seemed to accept the picture. In 1955, writing to the family of his friend Michele Besso, he said that for physicists the distinction between past, present and future is only a stubbornly persistent illusion.
