Nobody can feel a four-day difference in a galaxy they can’t see in detail, and nothing travels from Andromeda to Earth in less than 2.5 million years. So the walkers can’t disagree about anything they could ever check. What they disagree about is which events on Andromeda are simultaneous with their meeting, and relativity says both of them are entitled to their answer.

Penrose made the point vivid in *The Emperor’s New Mind* (1989). Suppose that, on a day inside the gap, the Andromedans decide to send a fleet toward Earth. For the walker heading toward Andromeda, that decision is already in the past. For the walker heading away, it is still in the future. If the future were open, undecided until it happens, then whether the fleet exists “now” would depend on which way someone in a street on Earth happens to be walking.

The window widens with speed. Someone passing at nearly the speed of light could count as “now” any moment of Andromeda’s history within 2.5 million years either side, which is exactly the light-travel time. Beyond that lies the light cone, and inside it the order of events is fixed for everyone.
