General relativity replaces the rigid block of special relativity with a curved one. Near a mass, the block is shaped so that clocks closer in tick more slowly than clocks farther out, and anything left alone follows the straightest available path through it. That is all gravity is, on this view: geometry.

The effect is small near Earth but not negligible. A GPS satellite is higher than we are, so its clock runs fast, and it moves quickly, so its clock runs slow. The first effect wins by about 38 microseconds a day. Engineers set the satellite clocks slightly slow before launch to compensate.

Near a black hole the bending is extreme. In coordinates that behave well at the horizon, each local light cone tips inward. At the horizon, light pointed straight out just hovers. Inside, every direction into the future leads to the singularity, which is why nothing gets out: the way out lies in the past.
