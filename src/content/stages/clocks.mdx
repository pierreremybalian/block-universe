The twin “paradox” is only a paradox if you expect the two twins to be in the same situation. They are not. Home stays in one frame the whole time; the traveler changes frames at the turnaround. In the block, that difference is visible: one worldline is straight, the other has a corner.

A clock measures the length of its own path through spacetime, and spacetime has an unusual geometry. On a map, a straight line is the shortest route between two places. In spacetime, a straight worldline between two events is the longest in time: any detour, out and back, adds distance and subtracts time. The traveler’s path measures 6.4 years; home’s, between the same two events, measures 10.6.

The turnaround is instant here, which no rocket could survive. Real journeys accelerate gradually, which rounds off the corner but does not change the answer much: the bent path is always shorter. And the effect is not limited to rockets. Every clock that moves, or sits higher or lower in a gravitational field, keeps its own time, and our satellites have to be built around that fact.
