Einstein asked how two distant clocks could be said to read the same time. His answer was operational: send light between them and assume it takes as long to go as to come back. Do that from a moving train and from the platform and you get two different answers about which distant events are simultaneous. Neither answer is privileged.

In the block, that disagreement is a difference of angle. Each observer’s “now” is a slice through spacetime, tilted by their speed. The slices all pass through the place where the observers meet, and fan apart with distance: at three light-hours, a slice tilted for half the speed of light is an hour and a half off.

What every observer agrees on is the light cone, and with it the order of any two events that could be cause and effect. Only events that nothing could connect, separated more in space than in time, can swap order.
