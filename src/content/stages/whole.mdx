Light takes time to travel, so looking out is looking back. Everything we observe, from the Moon to the microwave background, lies on one surface in spacetime: the set of events whose light is arriving here, now. That surface is our past light cone.

In an expanding universe the cone has a surprising shape. Near us it widens as expected. But light from the very early universe was emitted when everything was much closer together, so the cone narrows again toward the Big Bang. Measured in the distances things had when their light set out, it is a teardrop, widest at about 5.9 billion light-years.

None of this privileges our position. Every event in the block has its own past light cone. If the block universe is right, all of those events, and all of those cones, are equally real.
